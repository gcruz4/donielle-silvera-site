02 · About

"The church has office hours. Grief doesn't. I decided a long time ago to work the second schedule, and to keep the door open to anyone who needs it."

Donielle Silvera didn't choose pastoral work so much as agree to it. Ordained through [Denomination] after training at [Seminary Name], he spent the next twenty-two years learning that most of ministry happens outside the sanctuary: in driveways, break rooms, and hospital hallways, at the hours nobody puts on a church calendar.

For fifteen of those years he led a congregation at [Church Name], preaching on Sundays and doing the quieter work the rest of the week: premarital counseling at kitchen tables, hospital visits before dawn, funerals for families who needed someone steady in the room. In [Year], he stepped out from behind the pulpit to do that work full-time, not because the congregation stopped mattering, but because the need for it clearly extended past one building's walls.

He does not require you to join anything, believe a particular list of things, or explain yourself first. He shows up, he listens, and he stays as long as it takes.

In recent years he's added certified life-coaching training to that pastoral foundation: practical, goal-oriented guidance for the ordinary seasons between crises, still drawn from scripture rather than a secular self-help framework.

Based in Los Alamitos, CA, and travels regionally for weddings, funerals, and speaking.
